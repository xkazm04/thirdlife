WHAT THE DESIGNER NOW SAYS THAT IT DID NOT BEFORE (prompt diffs, for the owner)
- Before (26 lines): "Write the brief ... Be concrete and small: a mini-game that can be built and verified by a bot,
  not a wish list. Do not invent features the sentence does not ask for." Output: rules/arena/ai/controls/mood.
- Now (foreman/prompts/designer.md):
  * The goal is "the most fun game the sentence can become, built in phases". "A small, correct game that nobody plays
    twice is a failure." Fill every gap with the most fun choice; never hand a decision back as a question.
  * Wow: "the player should read your pitch and think 'I did not say that, but yes, exactly that'". Deeper means more
    character and more play, not more systems.
  * The fun checklist it works through, each made concrete with numbers: the verb; a camera that serves the verb
    (named TL calls, figure at 0.2-0.3 of the frame, never a far top-down view); juice on the verbs that matter
    (shake, burst, sound, 3-6 frames of hit-stop the pack holds itself; a miss gets less); rivals with a name, a habit,
    reactions, difficulty = speed/reaction/aggression; a first minute that teaches by playing; stakes for the next
    round; an intensity curve; one twist.
  * Aspect cards for a 10-year-old and an expert: pitch (<=20 words), 3-7 aspects chosen for THIS game, each with an
    icon, title/line in words a child feels, a 60-word expert detail naming the engine call, and 2-3 alternatives that
    are other games, not tweaks. Word limits are hard and counted, with targets well under them (iteration 1).
  * A compact worked example ("a race with ducks": shallow vs deep).
  * Creativity: STANDARD serves the player's words; CRAZY keeps every word and adds 1-2 unexpected mechanics on top,
    each its own aspect with crazy:true, removable in one tap, still buildable; "not the standard twist made bigger:
    a crazy mechanic bends a rule the player takes for granted" (iteration 2).
  * Feasibility guard: the prompt carries engine/kernel/API.md sections 0 (start here), 12 (cameras), 13 (HUD, VFX,
    audio, steering) and 17 (what the kernel does not do), the list of all API sections, the 36 kit parts, and every
    card's summary plus its new `fun` lines. "Map a wish the engine lacks onto what it has"; TLHand is not game AI.
  * Redesign: the previous brief and every reaction (KEEP / DROP / CHOSE alternative / the player's note) with rules
    for each; a dropped aspect may not come back (checked, not only asked).
- Planner (planner.md): sees the pitch, the aspect cards and the player's reactions ("they win over the brief");
  "the game must be fun, not only correct" (each fun ingredient lands in the contract of the package that owns it);
  the camera serves the verb from the first package; 2-5 phases the player tries, the first the smallest thing that
  plays, each with a try line (<=12 words).
- Critic (critic.md): "You judge legibility and consistency, not fun" is gone; a `fun_promise` rubric item joins the
  default four whenever there is a promise: the try line of the phase the milestone's last package is in, else the
  pitch ("the frames show this promise happening, not only a place where it could").
