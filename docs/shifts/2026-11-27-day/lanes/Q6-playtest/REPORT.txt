HOW I PLAYED (read this before trusting anything below)
- The editor binary (Godot 4.7.2 console exe, not --headless) in a 1920x1080 window on res://boot/boot.tscn, TL_STYLE=crisp,
  each run on a scratch Home (TL_HOME_BUILD_DIR). Not the exported exe.
- I have no hands. Input went through the lanes' hooks: TL_EXE_KEYS (keys held and let go at physics frames),
  TL_BUILD_DEVICES (the pointer at a pixel or a world point, LMB, RMB, keys), TL_COMMAND_DEVICES (the sentence, Enter,
  the captures). They make the same Input events a keyboard and a mouse make. I never turned the camera with the mouse
  in play, so every walk is W A S D against a fixed camera. A person steers better than my scripts; a person also
  meets everything below sooner.
- Not done: typing into a text field (run name and message kept their defaults), the mouse wheel (the hook has none),
  a stamp of my own course (the box needs the wheel), X export / I import, sound (I cannot hear; the files under
  kernel/world/build and kernel/world/home hold no audio call, though the kernel has TLAudio for games, so I expect
  the build tools and the rules to be silent).
- Step 1 was done blind: all ten tools once from the hotbar and its hint line only, then BUILD.md. BUILD.md is a
  developer file (API tables, file lists); the keys table in it is the only part a player could use. There is no
  player page.
- ONE real LLM call (claude-opus-5-5 through foreman world-edit). No fix call (R) was sent.
- Side effect on the machine: the system clipboard now holds a TL1 share code (Ctrl+C in the stamp tool).

and above all the street lamps at night, all read as one hand with no effort from me. The Rules tool's presets and
the card's sentences ("when you press E in Door 1: open Door 1 / when you leave Door 1: close Door 1 / [ ] closes

empty Home Ctrl+V gave "pasted Garden gate (7 zones, 8 rules)" and one click placed 243 ops. Saved / unsaved is shown.

VERDICTS, ONE PER THESIS CLAIM
- Does building look good without effort?  YES. Every tool I got to work made something that belongs in the picture.
  The exceptions are small: a stamp sits a curb above the grass, and what the model called a garden is a stone box.
- Does a build do something without code?  YES ON A BLOCK FLOOR, NOT RELIABLY ON GROUND. Seven presets: seven digits,
  seven clicks or drags, one Enter, and I had start, coin, door, checkpoint, pad, hazard and finish. Two of the seven then misfired for a
  reason that is invisible in the tool (item 6), and two of them (door, hazard) are invisible in play (item 8).
- Does the LLM build what was asked?  THE PARTS YES, THE PLAY NO. In 77 s it placed what the sentence named and wired
  it correctly. It could not be entered without a hand edit, and the check that exists to catch that passed it.
- Is playing it fun for a minute?  NOT YET. Garden gate is 3 seconds and asks nothing. Pad hop is a loop or 3.4
  seconds. Tower climb I could not climb. My own course was 6 seconds. The dressing (clock, card, confetti, ghost) is
  ahead of the courses. The minute I enjoyed was building the course, not running it. No audio call in the rules' code.
- Does night make you want to build lamps?  YES, AND YOU CANNOT. Night itself is pretty (moon with a halo, stars,
  long shadows) and bright enough that nothing needs a lamp. Two street lamps by the gate changed the scene more than
  anything else I placed all day: a warm pool on the stone, the fence lit from one side, coins and pads glowing.
  The lamp is locked behind three dungeon plays and N works only in build mode, so a newcomer may never see it.

AGAINST THE RESEARCH: three things Minecraft, Roblox and Fortnite Creative do that a newcomer here misses first
1. The body fits the build (Minecraft; R1 section 2). Steve jumps 1.25 blocks and walks through a 2-high door, so
   everything anyone builds can be walked. Here the jump is 0.87 of a block, a 2-high door stops him, a stamp makes a
   curb, and two of three starters cannot be finished by walking. The thesis line "play it at once" rests on this.
2. You can build where you stand, with everything, and the screen answers a click (Minecraft's hotbar, Roblox
   Studio's toolbar, Fortnite Creative's device gallery; R2 finding 2: the skill floor kills building games). Here
   the tabs are not clickable, four tools are dead for 350 m, lots are unmarked, and the reach limit is silent.
3. Devices show themselves and say their key (Fortnite Creative: a visible device, "E" over it, a hazard that looks
   like one; Hytale's trigger volumes are edited as shapes but played as things; R1 finding 7). Here a door is two
   planks in a wall, a hazard is nothing, and the only E on the screen is about entering a game.
   A fourth, sooner than all three but I could only read it in the code: sound. I found no audio call for a placed
   block, a coin, a door or a finish.
