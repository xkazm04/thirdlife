WHAT I BUILT, AND WHY IT MOVES TOWARDS BOARD 2

One idea: a cel picture is flat fields of paint with cut edges. So every edge in my scope (a band of light, a cast
shadow, a field of the land, a stratum, a mark) is a cut that is one pixel soft at any distance, and nothing between
two edges is a gradient.

1. The cel material model, in files of its own (toon.model = "cel" -> engine/style/shaders/toon_cel.gdshader; helper
   engine/style/cel/cel_surfaces.gd). toon.gdshader, water.gdshader and style.json are byte for byte the base's.
   - Light: flat bands (lit | shade | deep); the band edge is one pixel wide by the screen's own derivatives (a fixed
     softness was a gradient metres wide on rolling land). A cast shadow is cut at half of the sun's shadow: a line,
     however the shadow map is filtered. The shade keeps the paint's chroma. No hatching, no mottle, no rim.
   - Level land takes one ambient and no bounce; land that leans less than a dead zone is lit as level, so a meadow is
     one lit field and a hill's far flank is one shade band (no shards).
2. Land painted by numbers. The terrain's pigment is a blend of palette paints per 3 m vertex; every fragment takes the
   nearest of eight paints (grass, grass_dark, grass_dry, dirt, sand, rock, cliff, rock_warm), with a wobbly edge.
   Rock never wins on level ground and earth never on a steep face. On top:
   - the road: a dark verge thick with tufts in two sizes where grass meets it, blades of turf that hang over the
     road's edge, a darker rim on its own side, light and damp patches of 2 to 8 m, pebbles;
   - the meadow: patches of the dry-grass paint (the board's warm olive) against the green, running on over the far
     plain to the horizon; dark grass under every scattered tree and bush; sparse dark tufts (none lighter than
     the grass, as on the board) that end at 100 m and lean in the wind near the eye; handfuls of flowers. A tuft
     is paint on the ground, so from the visitor's low eye it lay flat as a dash; since 18:25 each tuft points
     away from the eye at its own place and is drawn longer on the ground the lower the eye looks along it (up to
     2.6 times, never beyond its cell): they stand upright anywhere on the screen, as the board's do, and do not
     turn when the eye turns. Since 18:42 (the Director's hour, "tufts growing tall on grazing slopes"): the cause
     was a bank that faces the eye, which shows the same paint longer than level ground does; the shader now
     measures how long a metre of the ground under the tuft is on the screen and draws the tuft that much shorter
     (down to 0.4, never longer than on level ground). Before and after: stills/tufts13_before_after.jpg (the
     right half of 13_lot_river_road; the tall blades at the right edge are tufts like the others now).
3. Bluffs, mesas, rock fields: flat strata in world height (4.4 m beds) in two alternating tones with a drawn seam,
   at any distance; about one bed in four is paler (cream at 0.4 in the sun, a lighter rock in the shade); rock has a
   shade of its own. After integration 2 the rock paints were fitted to the board's mesas in numbers: lit 204,120,138
   (board 200,120,132; at integration 2 it was 246,152,140, a pale salmon), shade 168,108,114 (board 165,90,118).
4. Nature as clean forms, without one new triangle: a bush, a crown, a rock and a pine tier are drawn with the round
   thing's own normal (in the prepass too: the ink draws the outline, not a crease per facet) and have a top light;
   the skirt of every pine tier is cut in teeth (in its shadow and its outline too); scattered trees lean in the wind.
5. Timber and props: a painted highlight along the edges of box faces, strong in the sun, weak in the shade.
6. The river (water_cel.gdshader): two flat blues, light streaks that drift along the flow, a foam line at the real
   waterline and round every pier (from depth), large flat shapes of sky for a grazing eye only, and the sun's road:
   cream dashes towards the sun. Drawn before the ink pass, so contours lie over the water. After integration 2
   (the Director: "a pale lilac band with dark squiggles" from above): the channel is 114,108,198 against the board's
   118,109,195, the shallows end at 0.35 m of water (they were a quarter of the river), and the squiggles had a cause:
   the ribbon is two triangles a segment with a swelling width, so a line of constant UV.y is a zigzag; the streaks
   are now drawn in metres across taken back from the ribbon's own rule, and lie along the flow.
7. Drifting cloud shadows: round cut shapes (one in some cells of 200 m, longer along the wind) that fall on land,
   trees and yards alike. Until integration 2 they were a contour of noise, which drew the C-shaped stain the
   Director saw in 08, and they were placed in a pack's own space, so a game's yard cut them; both mended.
8. Rails along both sides of the main road, as the board shows (the Director's word of 14:35): presentation only, no
   collider, gaps at spur mouths, bridge, plots and lots, 9 m round every plot's gate, one open span in nine; in
   pieces that are not drawn beyond 108 m (thinner than a pixel there). A plot's flag and sign stand 1.3 m before
   the plot's own fence under crisp (detail.sign_out) instead of in its line.
9. Colours fitted to board 2 in numbers (sampled from the board's file), on the joined tree.

WHAT STILL FALLS SHORT OF BOARD 2 (my scope; honest)
- Pines. The board has rows and clusters of small pines along every field edge and horizon; the village has a few
  dozen. Denser pines are scatter (landscape.gd's random stream, colliders, hashes): ruled out of scope today.
- Tufts are paint on the ground. They stand upright on the screen and are one size across rolling land now, but
  they have no height: nothing hides behind one. Real standing tufts are geometry (a MultiMesh of cut cards), not
  tried today. Their behaviour in a walk was seen in the clip only, not measured; the crawl pan was not run again
  after the 18:42 cap.
- The meadow's green. The board's lit grass is 200,182,22 (lemon); ours is 168,180,28 after the owner's word
  "less lemon" at integration 1, with olive patches of the dry-grass paint. The board's darker rolling bands come
  from hills in shade; ours come from patches, cloud shadows and the hills' own shade, in that order.
- Bluffs. Lit and shade now sit on the board's numbers, but the board's mesas are stepped (a wide base, a narrower
  cap, thin dark lines); ours have the terrain's own shape and broader beds, and about one bed in four is paler,
  which the board does not show as strongly. Rock fields on gentle slopes read as pink slabs in the meadow.
- The river is right in colour and its streaks lie along the flow, but the board's river carries long pale
  lens shapes across its whole width and a darker far bank; ours has streaks of 2 to 9 m and a foam line.
- Thin cast shadows (rails on the road) have a sawtooth edge: the shadow filter under my cut (cel-astra's keys;
  four measured tries in PATCHES, cel-astra -3: quality 5 with blur 1.0 gives a smooth line).
- Rails: the visitor walks through them (no collider, as ruled), and their hard edges add shimmer in a slow pan
  (crawl table). Posts stand at fixed steps and the scatter does not know them: a bush stands in the row in
  views/05_nature_close.
- Timber and props: the edge highlight follows TLMesh's box records and is dropped on thin members (a member
  that is all edge); I did not go through the kit to list which parts carry none.
- Not measured: a still camera's crawl (0 is expected, not shown); the GPU cost of a better shadow filter.
