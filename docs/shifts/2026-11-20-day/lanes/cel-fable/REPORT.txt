WHAT I BUILT, AND WHY IT MOVES TOWARDS BOARD 2

One idea: a cel picture is flat fields of paint with cut edges. So every edge in my scope (a band of light, a cast
shadow, a field of the land, a stratum, a mark) is a cut that is one pixel soft at any distance, and nothing between
two edges is a gradient.

1. The cel material model, in files of its own (toon.model = "cel" -> engine/style/shaders/toon_cel.gdshader; helper
   engine/style/cel/cel_surfaces.gd). toon.gdshader, water.gdshader and style.json are byte for byte the base's.
   - Light: flat bands (lit | shade | deep); the band edge is one pixel wide by the screen's own derivatives (a fixed
     softness was a gradient metres wide on rolling land). A cast shadow is cut at half of the sun's shadow: a line,
     however the shadow map is filtered. The shade keeps the paint's chroma. No hatching, no mottle, no rim.
   - Level land takes one ambient and no bounce; land that leans less than a dead zone is lit as level, so a meadow is
     one lit field and a hill's far flank is one shade band (no shards).
2. Land painted by numbers. The terrain's pigment is a blend of palette paints per 3 m vertex; every fragment takes the
   nearest of eight paints (grass, grass_dark, grass_dry, dirt, sand, rock, cliff, rock_warm), with a wobbly edge.
   Rock never wins on level ground and earth never on a steep face. On top:
   - the road: a dark verge with thicker tufts where grass meets it, a darker rim on its own side, light and damp
     patches of 2 to 8 m, pebbles;
   - the meadow: patches of the dry-grass paint (the board's warm olive) against the green, running on over the far
     plain to the horizon; dark grass under every scattered tree and bush; sparse tufts (some sunlit) that end at
     100 m and lean in the wind near the eye; handfuls of flowers.
3. Bluffs, mesas, rock fields: flat strata in world height in two alternating tones with a drawn seam, at any distance;
   about one bed in four is cream (cream in the sun, a lighter rock in the shade); rock has a shade of its own (mauve
   under a blue shade light, as on the board).
4. Nature as clean forms, without one new triangle: a bush, a crown, a rock and a pine tier are drawn with the round
   thing's own normal (in the prepass too: the ink draws the outline, not a crease per facet) and have a top light;
   the skirt of every pine tier is cut in teeth (in its shadow and its outline too); scattered trees lean in the wind.
5. Timber and props: a painted highlight along the edges of box faces, strong in the sun, weak in the shade.
6. The river (water_cel.gdshader): two flat blues, light streaks that drift along the flow, a foam line at the real
   waterline and round every pier (from depth), large flat shapes of sky, and the sun's road: cream dashes towards
   the sun. Drawn before the ink pass, so contours lie over the water.
7. Drifting cloud shadows: large cut shapes that fall on land, trees and yards alike; they lay dark bands across the
   big fields.
8. Rails along both sides of the main road, as the board shows (the Director's word of 14:35): presentation only, no
   collider, gaps at spur mouths, bridge, plots and lots, one open span in nine.
9. Colours fitted to board 2 in numbers (sampled from the board's file), on the joined tree.
