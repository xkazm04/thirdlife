WHAT I BUILT, AND WHY IT MOVES TOWARD THE BOARD

The owner's word was "blurry, far below the concept's fidelity". In my scope the cause was one thing: last night's
marks were formulas (value noise stretched into fibres) and last night's nature was the kit's facets lit as round.
At 1080p a formula reads as a smear and a facet as a blob. Today every mark is a texture made fibre by fibre and
every piece of nature has the form a hand would give it. Ten things:

1. Six material textures (engine/style/dio/tex/*.res, 1024 px, mipmaps inside, anisotropic sampling; made by
   engine/style/dio/make_tex.py, baked by bake_tex.gd): flock (46 000 fibres), felted wool (15 000 long curly
   fibres, pills), clay (thumb dents with their prints, knife drags, crumbs), wood (growth lines, pores, knots),
   cast block (air holes of the pour), soil (9 000 crumbs, stones pressed in). Each texel is a slope, a tone and a
   height; the shader lights the slope. They load without an import step, in the editor binary and the exe alike.
2. Flocked ground. The land samples the flock twice (fibres, and coarser clumps turned by 37 degrees, so no tile
   shows), fibres of two dyes, places where it lies thinner. The sheet is cut clean against the earth: the pigment
   near the cut is un-mixed into dye and earth, so there is no sprayed halo, and the cut edge itself is ragged
   (fibres hang over it). Flock tufts stand over the whole meadow in drifts of two dyes, thickest near the roads,
   and mounds of dark flock lie round the foot of every tree. The earth is crumbs on rolled clay at two scales,
   so a road reads as earth from a walker's eye too.
3. Felt pines in tiers: every cone of the kit recipe becomes a felt skirt (puffed, a rim cut in lobes that hang,
   a rolled lip, a dark underside), felted wool wrapped around it without a seam.
4. Felt bushes with form: a bush is a cluster of felt balls rolled one by one and pressed together (a ring and one
   or two on top), darker where they meet. Crowns are balls of wool, the roadside tufts clumps of flock.
5. Clay: rocks are lumps rolled in the hand and pressed flat here and there; the mesas are slabs of rolled clay
   laid one on another with a rounded outline, a piece cut off each; rock pigment on a bank shows as clay slabs;
   clay pebbles lie in strews on the roads, grit is strewn all over them and stones lie on the river bank (real
   lumps; the drawn discs are gone). The roadsides are dressed with knee-high pines, felt balls and stones.
6. A resin river that mirrors: the cotton clouds stand in it, and the mirrored ray is walked through the picture
   drawn before the resin, so banks, bushes and pines stand in it too. The sun's glint is a soft pool, not sparks.
7. Wood: grain along every dowel and rail (three times as coarse on a dowel, and stronger, so it reads from the
   road); the village's plot fences are turned posts and round rails, and so is the row of rails along both sides of
   the main road
   (cel-fable's road_rails, switched on for the diorama at the second integration: `detail.road_rails`); a
   scaffold's square steel bars are drawn as turned wooden dowels and the arches as bent wood. After the second
   integration (Director: "pale and plasticky") every dowel is its own piece of wood: a tone and a warmth of its
   own, matt, in the fence's brown instead of the plank's cream. Measured against the board's scaffold (wood-like
   pixels of the same kind of patch): board mean 173,98,42; before 219,140,61 at its palest; now 193,113,49.
8. Paint: a painted box gets uneven cream edges where the brush did not keep to the edge and the primer shows
   (the striped beam, kerbs, signs), and brush strokes that read. A painted thing of plain clay is smooth.
9. Kit and walls: a wall or slab of class concrete is laid of cast blocks with pressed joints; the primitives the
   kernel makes for bodies (the grey slab walls of a simple pack: BoxMesh, SphereMesh) get the part's space, the
   clay texture and soft edges; chamfered boxes carry the face record, so they get the soft edge and the paint.
10. The colour key of the board: lit grass golden olive, pines dark, earth orange-tan; shade warmed so salmon
    rock in shade is plum, not magenta. Retuned twice against the partner's sun. The dye follows the land, not
    noise alone: deep green in the hollows and gold on the crowns (the land's own colours, by its curvature), golden
    on a slope that looks at the sun and cooler where it turns away (the shader). And a fault found on the way:
    with a golden dye the shader took the whole meadow for the brown halo of the road and painted every green of
    the land over with one dye; the test is measured against the dye now. Measured against the board (median of
    plain patches drawn by hand): the lit nap's HUE is the board's (board 39..46 degrees, ours 43..47; no pixel
    of the board's grass has green above red, it reads green beside its redder road); its VALUE is a fifth to
    30 % above the board's (board 177,134,32 and 190,149,40; ours 229,179,49 in 52_chase_b), and so is the
    road's (board 221,126,53; ours 255,168,68, red clipped). So the mustard is exposure, not dye: it is the
    grade's (dio-astra's roll-off, in its round 5; PATCHES dio-astra 8), and I did not darken the dye as well.
    My stills are taken under the light of the second integration, before that roll-off.

Geometry of the kit, collision, physics and every pack are untouched. Everything new is presentation, drawn from
the kit's own recipes, which stay the source of colliders, crown hulls and hashes, and it is all behind keys of
style_diorama.json: detail.tex, tex_felt_m, tex_clay_m, tex_relief, nature, pebbles, grit, tufts, dress, dowels,
dye_relief, road_rails; water.clouds, cloud, glint, mirror_world. A style without them (classic) loads no texture and
draws the recipes.

WHAT STILL FALLS SHORT OF THE BOARD (honest)
1. The rail fences along the road (cel-fable's row, in felt-wood here) have NO collider: the visitor runs through
   a rail. Every ninth span is open and the row stops at spurs, plots, the bridge and the river, but between two
   gaps he can still cross where a rail is drawn. IT SHOWS IN clip.mp4 at 5.4 s: the scripted visit leaves the
   road for the yard straight through a rail (frames checked). One line switches the rows off for the diorama:
   `detail.road_rails` out of style_diorama.json. A collider needs the landscape's `world_space` (not my files).
2. The terrain's own bluff and banks are shading: rock pigment drawn as clay slabs on the land's smooth slope.
   The mesas have real slabs; the land has not (its heightfield is the visitor's collider, not mine to cut).
   In shade the banks still read pink-plum against the olive meadow.
3. Felt has no fibre at its silhouette. A pine's skirt and a felt ball end in a clean line; the fuzz is in the
   texture and the sheen. Shells of fibre would give it and cost a multiple of the triangles.
4. From the road a scaffold's dowel is a few pixels wide: its grain is there up close (31_parkour_run) and
   averaged away at distance. The scaffolds read as wood by tone and roundness.
5. The crane and the lamp towers are still painted steel lattice (the board's crane is yellow too, but chunkier).
   Only a scaffold's bars are redrawn as dowels: the parkour yard's house frame and its ladders keep square bars
   in the pale plank colour, so beside the brown scaffolds they still read as cream plastic (07).
5b. The lit meadow is a fifth to 30 % brighter than the board's (same hue): mustard where the board is olive gold.
   See item 10 of what I built: the value is left to the grade, and if the grade does not take it, 20 % off
   `palette.grass`, `grass_dry` is a two-value change.
6. Roadside tufts are one clump of flock repeated 1400 times; the dressing pines are the kit pine at knee height.
   Variety is by turn and scale only.
7. The stripes of a beam are still boxes: the uneven cream edge is drawn inside each box's faces, the boundary
   between two boxes is still a straight line under it.
8. Hulls, prisms and lofts (wedges, roofs, the arch ribs) have no face record: hard edges, no painted edge.
9. The mirror in the river sees only what is on screen: a pine above the frame is not mirrored, the sky stands
   there instead. At the screen's edge the mirror fades.
10. Far land (beyond about 100 m) is tone only; the far plain between the village and the mesas is flat.
11. Ruts along the road (Director, 14:36): not built. Tried at 14:57: the village's land wrote the distance to the
    road's middle into its vertex colour and the shader pressed two ruts a wheel's width from it. The land's grid
    is 3 m: a distance interpolated over 3 m cannot carry a rut 0.3 m wide (nothing showed), so it was taken out
    again, whole. The road has grit strewn all over and pebbles in strews instead; the long knife drags of the
    clay texture run any way, not along the road.

BEHAVIOUR CHANGES (all presentation; none under TL_STYLE=classic)
- Under a style with `detail.nature` the village's and a pack's kit trees, rocks and bushes are other meshes than
  the recipe builds (more triangles, smooth normals); `tl_hash` and `tl_tris` of the mesh stay the recipe's.
- A felt tree's or bush's sun shadow is cast by its recipe, 16 % narrower: a shadow on the ground is a little
  smaller than the felt, and a felt tree does not shade itself.
- TLMesh: a chamfered box has one more vertex channel on its six main faces (UV2). Classic never reads it.
- A pack's picture (stand-in) is cached under another key per diorama switch; classic's key is unchanged.
- Under a style with `detail.road_rails` and felt nature, the road's rail rows (road_rails.gd, no colliders) are
  turned posts and round rails; `build()` and its arguments are unchanged, a style without felt nature gets the
  bars cel-fable wrote.
- Test expectations: one moved with its code, not loosened: dio.tex expects the picture key's new `|look<n>` part
  under the diorama (and still '' under classic).
