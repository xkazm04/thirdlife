# The Third Life cut of hockey: one page for the owner (lane K8, 25 Nov 2026)

**Nobody has played it.** Everything below was measured with scripted hands, not with a person. It cannot tell you whether
it is fun; only your hand can. What it can promise: every change from thirdjam is deliberate, has a reason you can read,
was measured before and after, and is one switch or one number to undo.

The Third Life cut is thirdjam's game (same rink, same skaters, same speeds, same bots' brain, same camera and keys) with
**four rules changed, two numbers changed and each level's opponents re-set**. The Regular cut beside it is thirdjam untouched.

## What you will feel, in the first minute

| # | Change | What you feel | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A goal must leave a blade | A breakaway ends with a press. Glide the puck at the net and it stops on the goal line; tap shoot and it is in. | In thirdjam a quarter of the goals were skated over the line; the shot, the deke and the one-timer were decorations. |
| 2 | The puck is taken with a key or not at all | Skating into a man who has the puck no longer gives it to you; press the stick lift, poke him or hit him. Your mates stop losing the puck to a bump. | Your ruling in thirdjam ("never by a body's touch") leaked one way: you could take pucks by skating into people, and so could the other side from your mates. |
| 3 | Knockdowns last about a second | You are back on your skates in a second, not two. You still lose the puck and your place. | A knockdown outlasted the play it was in. "A tackle is an event, not a rhythm." |
| 4 | Nobody glides faster than he can skate | Shift is a spend: the bar empty, you are at skating pace in half a second, and it must fill a quarter before it answers again. Pick the puck up at full speed and you slow to carrying pace. | In thirdjam a held Shift never ran out, and a puck picked up at speed was carried at speed, so "the carrier is slower" was true only from a standing start. |
| 5 | The first hit only staggers you | The hit that flattened you the instant you touched the puck now takes the puck and half your speed, and leaves you standing. | Most knockdowns came within half a second of winning the puck: nothing a hand can answer. |
| 6 | Rookie plays for the net | At rookie they shoot from the slot with a wind-up you can see, wide as often as not, and now and then lift your stick. Stand still and you lose. | Rookie was won 6 times in 10 by a player who never moved. "Easy by clumsiness, never by absence." |
| 7 | Pro presses; all-star has your legs | At pro they come from further off and their sticks are back on yours a second after a miss. At all-star nobody is faster than you by legs; they hunt you across the ice instead. | The kept rules made pro easier; the level gives the difficulty back by pressure. All-star was harder by speed. |

## What is cut from this cut

The brake key (the reversed stick stops in a quarter of the distance; nobody ever pressed it), the ability key (this cut has
no ability), the saucer as a key of its own (a held pass is power; the game lifts it when it must). The keys themselves are
the shell's to hide; nothing was removed from the game's code.

## What was tried and thrown away

| Tried | Why it went |
|---|---|
| A faster tap pass for everybody | More passes arrived, but every bot passed faster too and all-star collapsed. |
| The goal line closed only while the puck "rides" | It leaked: the puck bounced off the line and was pushed in. Rewritten (change 1). |
| Mates pass back twice as often | Changed almost nothing: the number is rarely what decides. |
| The give-and-go (a mate looks for you first for two seconds) | The puck did not come back more often. |
| Pro's bots shoot, deke and pass like all-star's | Pro got easier: a bot that winds up is a bot that gets stripped. |

## The ladder, beside thirdjam's

Wins, per cent, 200 seeds a cell (give or take about 3). thirdjam's own figure in brackets, measured the same way.

| Hand | Rookie | Pro | All-star |
|---|---|---|---|
| A player who never moves | 23.5 (60.5) | 9.0 (9.5) | 6.0 (5.5) |
| Planning hand, slow | 92.0 (98.0) | 55.5 (49.5) | 22.5 (23.5) |
| Planning hand, average | 99.5 (100) | 84.0 (85.0) | 66.5 (50.0) |
| Planning hand, quick | 100 (100) | 95.5 (95.5) | 88.0 (77.5) |
| thirdjam's scripted novice | 89.5 (97.5) | 15.0 (24.5) | 11.5 (10.5) |
| thirdjam's scripted regular | 98.5 (100) | 68.5 (75.5) | 48.0 (39.5) |
| thirdjam's scripted good | 100 (100) | 75.5 (76.0) | 56.0 (53.5) |

Read it as:
- **Rookie is no longer won standing still** (23.5 % against 60.5 %), and a newcomer still wins it nine times in ten.
- **Pro is where thirdjam's pro was** for the hand that plans (84 against 85).
- **All-star is easier than thirdjam's** for an average and a quick hand (66.5 against 50; 88 against 77.5). It lost its
  faster legs and its hit on your first touch, and has not yet been given that difficulty back by better play.
- My measuring hand is stronger than I meant it to be (its average setting wins 85 % of thirdjam's pro, where thirdjam's own
  average hand wins 75.5 %). The comparisons across the two cuts hold; "how hard is pro for an average person" does not
  follow from these figures.

## Which keys earn their place (the same hand at pro without one key, 200 seeds)

| Verdict | Keys |
|---|---|
| Carries the game | the shot (without it the hand wins 28 % instead of 84 %) |
| Matters | the charged shot, the stop, the sprint, the body check (each worth 5 to 10 points of wins) |
| Probably matters | carrying the puck on the far side from a defender |
| No worth shown alone | the tap shot, the stick lift, the poke, the brace, the spin, the one-timer |
| Costs the hand that uses it | the pass (about 5 points), the deke |

The lift, the poke and the check do one job (taking the puck), so each hides the others; together they were not measured.
The deke is the one key I would cut next; the one-timer is dead only because the pass is.

## What is still weak (said plainly)

1. **The pass does not pay.** About half the passes that reach a mate are lost by him before anything comes of them. Three
   tries, none kept. Your mates are thirdjam's plainest bots; they cannot keep a puck for a second under pro's pressure.
2. **Your own carry is as short as in thirdjam** (under a second for the measuring hands). Every loss now has a cause you
   can name; a carry does not yet last.
3. **Six keys show no worth alone** (tap shot, lift, poke, brace, spin, one-timer) and **the deke costs** (table above).
   Nothing was cut for it yet. And **all-star is easier than thirdjam's** (the ladder above).
4. **The team signal was never measured** (no scripted hand can time one).
5. **Nobody has played it.**

## Three questions only you can answer

1. At all-star, do you want "a beating massacre" (your words of 13 Nov) or "a tackle is an event" (the ruling of the same
   week)? The cut is built for the second: more hits come at harder levels, each one shorter and none on your first touch.
2. Should a breakaway end with a press? If gliding it in is a pleasure you want back, it is one switch.
3. Is pro the level you judged thirdjam at? The cut moves the two ends (rookie needs you, all-star has your legs) and leaves
   pro a little easier than thirdjam's for an average hand.
