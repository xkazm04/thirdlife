# Pip, the photo-finish judge

Pip used to sit on a tripod beside the village track. Then two runners tied, and the camera took off down the lane on a pair of orange bird legs to get a better angle. These days Pip judges the last stretch in person. When one striped wing goes up, the walker has until the shutter clicks to plant both feet. Move during the exposure and Pip sends you back to the last flag. Between exposures the long-legged judge has to crank its bellows open again, just long enough for a quick runner to steal a few strides.

## Modelling notes

- Block Pip at **4.5 m from sole to pennant tip** beside the 2.0 m walker. In the neutral front view, place the underside of the yellow housing at 1.8 m and its roof at 3.7 m. Leave daylight between the two shins.
- Start the head and torso as one chamfered yellow box about 2.1 m wide. Add the small rear wedge for a tail and plant a single leaning pennant mast in the roof. The mast and tail make the direction of travel legible in profile.
- Loft the lens from eight-sided rings. Two accordion folds can compress along the optical axis while the brow stays fixed to the casing. Give the plum lens one square amber glint so the walker can tell which way Pip is looking.
- Mount each signal wing on its own barrel hinge. Cut one broad ivory band across a rigid red paddle with a tapered tip. Offset the hinge from the casing far enough that the paddle clears the roof on its upswing.
- Build each leg from two tapered orange segments and a faceted knuckle. Fan three wedge toes from one ankle block and darken their tips. In the signal pose, shift the box over the planted foot before lifting the other knee.
- Use the golden style's `yellow` (#f2b230) on the shell, `flag_a` (#e8483a) and `curb_white` (#f3e8dc) on the wings, `orange` (#e8742c) on the legs and `steel_dark` (#4f4a60) around the lens. Let the plum outline and violet toon shadows describe the bevels.
