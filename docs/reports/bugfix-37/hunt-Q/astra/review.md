Paths beginning with `walks/`, `draw/`, or `terrain/` are relative to `engine/kernel/world/`. The numerical repro results below come from the existing candidate runs. The play conditions come from the callers and walk data.

## 1. b_ledger_collider — REAL in play

The walker room can be drawn with four walls the visitor can walk through after a next round. `tour_next_round` gives the room a 30 m radius and enables a 180-tick restart. The shipped test specifically checks for the walker room's restart. All four walls are inside that radius, and each setup creates fresh box shapes and visible meshes. (`walks/tour_next_round.json:8-12`, `engine/tools/selftest.sh:286-291`, `engine/kernel/examples/ex_walker/scripts/main.gd:20-26`, `engine/kernel/api/tl_bodies.gd:24-35`, `engine/kernel/api/tl_bodies.gd:70-79`.)

The viewer copies colliders only on **first** arrival, keeps the arrival flag through ledger, and places the replacement's visuals. Its explicit tag removal is for site scaffolding. Both primitive-copy paths give the server the source shape RID without retaining the resource. Both concave-copy paths retain their new shape. This explains why the room walls lose collision even when terrain still supports the visitor. ## 3. a_stage_input — REAL in play

Enter the running drive game while its bot is accelerating and the car can keep taking gas with W released. The boot village runs drive in bot mode. The holder selects the manifest's first bot, which calls `ctx.input.press("gas", …)` directly, and the car reads gas from that same input. Stage entry sets `bot_off` and enables devices on the existing input. The holder and life code skip the bot tick and continue sampling. `press` sets an indefinite hold, and an up physical key leaves the injected value intact. TLHand's `release_all()` requires a caller and the drive bot bypasses the hand entirely. Replay overrides the sampled value, so replay alone can mask the live-device failure. ## 5. a_camera_wall — REAL in play

The camera-only pine crown gives a reachable starting overlap. For a pine of horizontal scale 1, the solid trunk radius is 0.42 m. The crown has radius 1.73 m and begins about 1.81 m above the terrain at the trunk: its bottom is `2.2 × (0.42 / 0.46)` above a foot placed 0.20 m below the terrain. A visitor standing 1 m from the axis clears the trunk with the 0.35 m capsule. On level ground the 1.70 m look point is already only 0.11 m below the crown, inside the 0.30 m cast margin. The body checks layer 1 while the camera checks layers 1 and 4. The shipped canopy walk requests 120 pines. (`walks/wf_canopy.json:203-204`, `engine/kernel/world/landscape.gd:390-393`, `engine/kernel/world/landscape.gd:412-426`, `terrain/world_space.gd:18-19`, `terrain/world_space.gd:44-53`, `terrain/world_space.gd:129-148`, `engine/kernel/world/visitor.gd:86-94`, `engine/kernel/api/tl_chase_cam.gd:29-30`.)

Jumping makes the overlap deeper. At 60 Hz, with initial upward speed 6.93 m/s, gravity 24 m/s² and vertical anchor follow 0.18, the smoothed anchor rises about 0.87 m by the 21st jump tick. The look point reaches about 2.57 m. Even the largest generated pine (scale 1.35) starts its crown about 2.51 m above the trunk's terrain. Each avoidance cast starts at that same look point, so an initially overlapping crown can be skipped. The existing canopy regression moves the target horizontally at fixed height, and the candidate's wall repro demonstrates what the cast does when it starts overlapped. (`terrain/world_body.gd:26-27`, `terrain/world_body.gd:129-131`, `terrain/world_body.gd:149-158`, `terrain/world_body.gd:216-228`, `engine/kernel/world/visitor.gd:47-48`, `engine/kernel/world/visitor.gd:105-113`, `engine/kernel/api/tl_chase_cam.gd:154-159`, `engine/kernel/api/tl_chase_cam.gd:173-183`, `walks/walk_case.gd:344-364`, `docs/reports/astra-trial/d5/astra/cand_a.md:50`.)

Stand 1 m from the axis and pitch the boom 60° upward and away from the trunk. The underside initially holds the camera close. As a jump raises the look point past 1.81 m, the sphere overlaps the underside. ## 7. a_camera_cut_wall — REAL in play

A 600 px horizontal mouse delta in one tick turns the visitor 90°. The visitor accumulates relative motion until sampling. LOOK_X accepts the full analog value, yaw wraps, and only pitch is clamped. Live steering uses camera stiffness 1. The shipped `wf_feel` device script supplies exactly 600 px at tick 20 through the same mouse-event path.
